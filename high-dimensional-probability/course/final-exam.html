How the examination works

Arrive at the start of your booked appointment. The instructor will write the selected problem on the board; you will present your solution and answer questions about it. The 20 minutes cover both problems and all questions. A late arrival does not extend the appointment or delay the next student.

Problems are drawn uniformly at random from the eight published problems, without repetition within your exam. Prepare all parts of every problem. You should be able to explain why each step is valid, where the assumptions are used, and the cases or examples requested in the statement. You may use any theorem or fact explicitly supplied on the sheet without proving it.

If you cannot solve a selected problem, you may request a replacement. The replacement is drawn at random from the problems not yet drawn, and costs 5 points. You may begin a problem and then request a replacement; time already spent is part of the same 20-minute limit. A skipped attempt does not also receive a presentation score. There are at most two scored presentations, and at most eight distinct problems can be drawn. If there are no undrawn problems left, no further replacement is possible.

Scoring rubric

Each retained presentation is scored out of 25.

Points Standard
25 A complete, correct, well-explained solution to all parts, with convincing answers to follow-up questions.
20 A complete, correct presentation, but difficulty answering follow-up questions about the argument.
15 Reaches a correct solution but cannot fully explain or justify important steps.
10 Makes meaningful progress, but the solution remains incomplete or time runs out.
0–5 Little or no correct mathematical progress; an unattempted presentation receives 0.

The first three levels distinguish mastery, a correct prepared presentation, and a correct conclusion with gaps in its justification. The instructor uses the mathematical work shown to assess incomplete presentations.

If the presentation scores are \(s_1,s_2\) and \(r\) replacements were requested, the exam score is \[ \max\{0,s_1+s_2-5r\}\quad\text{out of }50. \] For example, two 25-point presentations after one replacement earn 45/50. Two 20-point presentations without a replacement earn 40/50. A second presentation that is not attempted before time expires earns 0.

Problem statements

Download the printable problem sheet (PDF)

Throughout, \(C,c>0\) denote absolute constants, whose values may change. All logarithms are natural. Write \(B_p^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\|x\|_p\leq1\}\), and, for a nonempty bounded set \(T\) in a Euclidean space, write \(w(T)=\mathbb E\sup_{t\in T}\langle g,t\rangle\) for standard Gaussian \(g\). A standard Gaussian matrix has independent \(N(0,1)\) entries; \(\|A\|_F^2=\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}^2\) denotes its squared Frobenius norm.

1. Estimating a trace without reading the matrix

Let \(A\) be a nonzero positive semidefinite \(n\times n\) matrix. Suppose that we can compute \(Av\) for a chosen vector \(v\), but cannot read the entries of \(A\) directly. Let \(g\sim N(0,I_n)\) and let \(\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^n\) have independent uniform sign coordinates.

  1. Show that \(g^{\mathsf T}Ag\) and \(\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}A\varepsilon\) are unbiased estimators of \(\operatorname{tr}A\), and prove \[ \operatorname{Var}(g^{\mathsf T}Ag)=2\|A\|_F^2, \qquad \operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon^{\mathsf T}A\varepsilon) =2\sum_{i\ne j}A_{ij}^2. \]
  2. Choose either estimator. Form \(b\) independent batches, each containing \(k\) independent copies, and let \(\widehat\tau\) be a median of the \(b\) batch averages. For \(0<\delta,\rho<1\), prove that \[ k\geq C\delta^{-2},\qquad b\geq C\log(2/\rho) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \mathbb P\{|\widehat\tau-\operatorname{tr}A| >\delta\operatorname{tr}A\}\leq\rho. \] Thus only \(O(\delta^{-2}\log(2/\rho))\) matrix-vector products are needed, independently of \(n\).

Hint. Use positivity of the eigenvalues to bound the variance relative to \((\operatorname{tr}A)^2\), then control how many batch averages are inaccurate.

2. Gaussian concentration after a nonlinear map

Let \(A\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\) be a fixed matrix whose rows have Euclidean norm one. For independent \(g,h\sim N(0,I_n)\), set \[ X(g)=\tanh(Ag),\qquad \kappa=\mathbb E\tanh^2(Z),\quad Z\sim N(0,1),\qquad r=\frac{m}{\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^2}, \] where \(\tanh\) acts coordinatewise.

  1. Prove that \(\mathbb E\|X(g)\|_2^2=m\kappa\) and, for every \(t>0\), \[ \mathbb P\left\{ \left|\frac{\|X(g)\|_2^2}{m}-\kappa\right|>t \right\}\leq2e^{-crt^2}. \]
  2. Prove that, for every \(t>0\), \[ \mathbb P\left\{ \left|\frac{\langle X(g),X(h)\rangle}{m}\right|>t \right\}\leq2e^{-crt^2}. \] Deduce that, for \(0<\varepsilon<1\), with probability at least \(1-6e^{-cr\varepsilon^2}\), \[ \frac{|\langle X(g),X(h)\rangle|} {\|X(g)\|_2\|X(h)\|_2}\leq\varepsilon. \] The denominator is nonzero almost surely.

Hint. Use \(|\tanh u|\leq1\), \(0\leq\tanh'(u)\leq1\), and the oddness of \(\tanh\). The coordinates of \(X(g)\) need not be independent.

3. The ground-state energy of a Gaussian spin system

For \(n\geq2\), let \((Z_{ij})_{1\leq j<i\leq n}\) be independent standard normal random variables. For \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n\), define \[ H_\sigma=\sum_{i>j}Z_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j, \qquad E_n=\min_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n}H_\sigma. \]

  1. Prove that \(\mathbb EE_n=-\mathbb E\max_\sigma H_\sigma\) and that \(-\mathbb EE_n\leq Cn^{3/2}\).
  2. Construct signs successively by taking \(\sigma_1=1\) and choosing \(\sigma_i\) to maximize \(\sigma_i\sum_{j<i}Z_{ij}\sigma_j\). Condition on the previous rows to show that the expected value attained is \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\sum_{i=2}^n\sqrt{i-1}\). Deduce \(cn^{3/2}\leq-\mathbb EE_n\).
  3. Prove that \[ \mathbb P\left\{\left|\frac{E_n}{\mathbb EE_n}-1\right|>\delta\right\} \leq2e^{-c\delta^2 n}\qquad(\delta>0). \] In particular, \(E_n/\mathbb EE_n\to1\) in probability.

Facts you may use. For centered Gaussian variables with variances at most \(v^2\), \(\mathbb E\max_{1\leq j\leq N}X_j\leq v\sqrt{2\log N}\), without any independence assumption.

4. Recovering a signal from noisy measurements

An unknown signal \(x\) belongs to a known nonempty compact set \(T\subset\mathbb R^n\). We observe \[ y=Ax+\eta, \] where \(A\) is an \(m\times n\) standard Gaussian matrix and \(\eta\in\mathbb R^m\) is a fixed noise vector. Reconstruct \(x\) by choosing any measurable constrained least-squares solution \[ \widehat x\in\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{z\in T}\|Az-y\|_2. \] The expectation below is over \(A\); \(x\) and \(\eta\) are fixed.

  1. Prove that \[ \mathbb E\|\widehat x-x\|_2 \leq\frac{Cw(T)+2\|\eta\|_2}{\sqrt m}. \]
  2. If the only prior information is \(\|x\|_1\leq R\) for \(R>0\), take \(T=RB_1^n\) and deduce \[ \mathbb E\|\widehat x-x\|_2 \leq\frac{CR\sqrt{\log(2n)}+2\|\eta\|_2}{\sqrt m}. \]

Hint. Apply the following Gaussian deviation theorem to \(T-T\): for a compact symmetric set \(S\) containing \(0\), \[ \mathbb E\sup_{h\in S} \left|\|Ah\|_2-\sqrt m\|h\|_2\right|\leq Cw(S). \] You may use this theorem without proof.

5. Random sections of the octahedron

Let \(1\leq d<n\), let \(A\) be a \(d\times n\) standard Gaussian matrix, and let \(E=\ker A\). All radii and diameters below are Euclidean.

  1. Put \(T_r=B_1^n\cap rB_2^n\). If \(n^{-1/2}\leq r\leq1\), prove \[w(T_r)\leq C\sqrt{\log(enr^2)}.\] You may use \(\mathbb E(\sum_{i=1}^s(g_i^*)^2)^{1/2} \leq C\sqrt{s\log(en/s)}\), where \(g_1^*\geq\cdots\geq g_n^*\) are the decreasing rearrangement of \(|g_1|,\ldots,|g_n|\).
  2. Prove the Garnaev–Gluskin bound \[ \mathbb E\operatorname{diam}(B_1^n\cap E) \leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log(en/d)}{d}}. \]

Hint. Apply the Gaussian deviation theorem to \(T_r\) for a suitable \(r\). You may use the following form without proof: for compact symmetric \(S\) containing \(0\) and \(u\geq0\), with probability at least \(1-2e^{-u^2}\), \[ \sup_{h\in S}\left|\|Ah\|_2-\sqrt d\|h\|_2\right| \leq C_0\big[w(S)+u\operatorname{rad}(S)\big], \qquad \operatorname{rad}(S)=\sup_{h\in S}\|h\|_2. \]

6. A large Euclidean section of the \(\ell^1\)-ball

Let \(G\) be an \(n\times k\) standard Gaussian matrix, where \(1\leq k\leq n\), and put \(\mu=\mathbb E|g|=\sqrt{2/\pi}\) for \(g\sim N(0,1)\).

  1. Prove that there are absolute constants \(c_0,c_1>0\) such that, if \(k\leq c_0n\), then with probability at least \(1-2e^{-c_1n}\), \[ \frac{\mu}{2}n\|v\|_2\leq\|Gv\|_1\leq2\mu n\|v\|_2 \qquad\text{for every }v\in\mathbb R^k. \]
  2. Deduce that, for \(k\leq c_0n\) (reducing the absolute constants if needed), with probability at least \(1-2e^{-cn}\) the \(k\)-dimensional subspace \(F=\operatorname{range}(G)\) satisfies \[ c\sqrt n\|z\|_2\leq\|z\|_1\leq\sqrt n\|z\|_2 \qquad\text{for every }z\in F. \]

Hint. For a fixed unit vector \(v\), use Gaussian concentration for \(\|Gv\|_1\), then pass from a sufficiently fine net to the whole sphere. You may use the Gaussian singular-value estimates from the course.

7. Generic chaining on a binary tree

For a finite metric space \((T,d)\), define \[ \gamma_2(T,d)=\inf_{(T_j)}\sup_{t\in T} \sum_{j\geq0}2^{j/2}d(t,T_j), \qquad |T_0|=1,\quad |T_j|\leq2^{2^j}\ (j\geq1). \] You may use Talagrand’s theorem: for a centered Gaussian process with canonical metric \(d(s,t)=(\mathbb E|X_s-X_t|^2)^{1/2}\), \[ c\gamma_2(T,d)\leq\mathbb E\max_{t\in T}X_t\leq C\gamma_2(T,d). \]

Let \(T=\{0,1\}^m\), \(m\geq1\), be the leaves of a binary tree of depth \(m\). Attach independent standard Gaussian variables \(g_e\) to its edges and put \(X_t=\sum_{e\in[\varnothing,t]}g_e\).

  1. If \(\ell(s,t)\) is the length of the common prefix of two leaves, prove \(d(s,t)^2=2(m-\ell(s,t))\).
  2. Construct an admissible sequence by representing every prefix of length \(\min\{m,2^j\}\) at level \(j\geq1\) by one leaf, and taking a single leaf at level \(0\). Prove directly that \(\gamma_2(T,d)\leq Cm\).
  3. Starting at the root, repeatedly follow the edge with the larger Gaussian label. Use independence of unexplored edges to show that the expected value at the resulting leaf is \(m/\sqrt\pi\). Deduce \(\gamma_2(T,d)\geq cm\) and \(cm\leq\mathbb E\max_tX_t\leq Cm\).

Hint. If \(a,b\) are independent standard normals, \(\max(a,b)=(a+b+|a-b|)/2\). In part 2, bound the nonzero sum by \(C\sqrt m\sum_{2^j<m}2^{j/2}\), keeping the level-zero term.

8. A quadratic kernel and generic chaining

Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_n>0\), put \(D=\operatorname{diag}(a_1,\ldots,a_n)\), and define the ellipsoid \(E=DB_2^n\) and its quadratic lift \[ \Phi(x)=xx^{\mathsf T},\qquad \mathcal K=\{\Phi(x):x\in E\}. \] Equip \(\mathcal K\) with the Frobenius metric, induced by \(\langle A,B\rangle_F=\operatorname{tr}(A^{\mathsf T}B)\). Let \(G\) be an \(n\times n\) standard Gaussian matrix.

  1. Prove that \(\langle\Phi(x),\Phi(y)\rangle_F=\langle x,y\rangle^2\). Show that \(X_x=x^{\mathsf T}Gx\), \(x\in E\), is a centered Gaussian process with covariance \(\mathbb EX_xX_y=\langle x,y\rangle^2\), and that its canonical pseudometric satisfies \[ d(x,y)^2=\|x\|_2^4+\|y\|_2^4-2\langle x,y\rangle^2 =\|\Phi(x)-\Phi(y)\|_F^2. \]
  2. Prove the sharp estimate \[ c\left(\max_i a_i\right)\left(\sum_i a_i^2\right)^{1/2} \leq\gamma_2(\mathcal K,\|\cdot\|_F) \leq C\left(\max_i a_i\right)\left(\sum_i a_i^2\right)^{1/2}. \]

You may use Talagrand’s theorem from Problem 7 for compact Euclidean sets and their continuous Gaussian processes.

Hint. For the lower bound, set \(H=(G+G^{\mathsf T})/2\) and consider a column of \(DHD\) corresponding to the largest \(a_i\).
