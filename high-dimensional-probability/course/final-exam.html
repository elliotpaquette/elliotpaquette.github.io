How the examination works

Arrive at the start of your booked appointment. The instructor will write the selected problem on the board; you will present your solution and answer questions about it. The 20 minutes cover both problems and all questions. A late arrival does not extend the appointment or delay the next student.

Problems are drawn uniformly at random from the eight published problems, without repetition within your exam. Prepare all parts of every problem. You should be able to explain why each step is valid, where the assumptions are used, and the cases or examples requested in the statement. You may use any theorem or fact explicitly supplied on the sheet without proving it.

If you cannot solve a selected problem, you may request a replacement. The replacement is drawn at random from the problems not yet drawn, and costs 5 points. You may begin a problem and then request a replacement; time already spent is part of the same 20-minute limit. A skipped attempt does not also receive a presentation score. There are at most two scored presentations, and at most eight distinct problems can be drawn. If there are no undrawn problems left, no further replacement is possible.

Scoring rubric

Each retained presentation is scored out of 25.

Points Standard
25 A complete, correct, well-explained solution to all parts, with convincing answers to follow-up questions.
20 A complete, correct presentation, but difficulty answering follow-up questions about the argument.
15 Reaches a correct solution but cannot fully explain or justify important steps.
10 Makes meaningful progress, but the solution remains incomplete or time runs out.
0–5 Little or no correct mathematical progress; an unattempted presentation receives 0.

The first three levels distinguish mastery, a correct prepared presentation, and a correct conclusion with gaps in its justification. The instructor uses the mathematical work shown to assess incomplete presentations.

If the presentation scores are \(s_1,s_2\) and \(r\) replacements were requested, the exam score is \[ \max\{0,s_1+s_2-5r\}\quad\text{out of }50. \] For example, two 25-point presentations after one replacement earn 45/50. Two 20-point presentations without a replacement earn 40/50. A second presentation that is not attempted before time expires earns 0.

Problem statements

Download the printable problem sheet (PDF)

Throughout, \(C,c>0\) denote absolute constants, whose values may change. All logarithms are natural. Write \(B_p^n=\{x\in\mathbb R^n:\|x\|_p\leq1\}\), and, for a nonempty bounded set \(T\) in a Euclidean space, write \(w(T)=\mathbb E\sup_{t\in T}\langle g,t\rangle\) for standard Gaussian \(g\). Covering numbers have centers in the set being covered.

1. Gaussian quadratic forms and effective dimension

Let \(A\) be a nonzero real symmetric \(n\times n\) matrix, let \(g\sim N(0,I_n)\), and set \(Q=g^{\mathsf T}Ag-\operatorname{tr}A\).

  1. Diagonalize \(A\) and compute \(\mathbb E e^{\theta Q}\). Show that \[ \log\mathbb E e^{\theta Q}\leq C\theta^2\|A\|_F^2 \quad\text{if }|\theta|\leq c/\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}. \]
  2. Optimize the exponential Markov bound to prove, for \(t>0\), \[ \mathbb P\{|Q|\geq t\}\leq 2\exp\left[-c\min\left\{\frac{t^2}{\|A\|_F^2}, \frac{t}{\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}}\right\}\right]. \]
  3. Suppose \(A\succeq0\) and define \(r_2(A)=(\operatorname{tr}A)^2/\|A\|_F^2\) and \(r_\infty(A)=\operatorname{tr}A/\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). Express the relative-error bound at \(t=\varepsilon\operatorname{tr}A\) in terms of these quantities. Explain the two regimes using an orthogonal projection of rank \(r\), and check that \(r_\infty(A)\leq r_2(A)\leq\operatorname{rank}A\).

Hint. For \(|u|\leq1/2\), \(-\log(1-u)-u\leq Cu^2\). The argument must allow negative eigenvalues in parts 1 and 2.

2. One random map for a union of subspaces

Let \(V_1,\ldots,V_M\) be linear subspaces of \(\mathbb R^n\), each of dimension at most \(k\), where \(1\leq k\leq n\) and \(M\geq1\). Let \(G\) be an \(m\times n\) matrix of independent \(N(0,1)\) entries. Fix \(\varepsilon,\rho\in(0,1)\) and put \(T=\bigcup_{j=1}^M V_j\).

  1. For a fixed \(d\)-dimensional subspace \(W\), \(d\geq1\), use a \(1/4\)-net of its unit sphere and Problem 1 to prove \[ \mathbb P\left\{\sup_{x\in W\cap S^{n-1}} \left|m^{-1}\|Gx\|_2^2-1\right|>\varepsilon\right\} \leq 2\exp(Cd-cm\varepsilon^2). \]
  2. Deduce that, if \(m\geq C\varepsilon^{-2}(k+\log(M/\rho))\), then with probability at least \(1-\rho\), \[ (1-\varepsilon)\|x-y\|_2^2\leq m^{-1}\|G(x-y)\|_2^2 \leq(1+\varepsilon)\|x-y\|_2^2 \quad\text{for every }x,y\in T. \] Explain why controlling each \(V_j\) separately does not by itself prove this.
  3. Apply the result to all vectors with at most \(s\) nonzero coordinates, \(1\leq s\leq n\), to obtain the sufficient bound \[ m\geq C\varepsilon^{-2} \left(s\log\frac{en}{s}+\log\frac1\rho\right). \]

Facts you may use. A unit sphere in dimension \(d\) has a \(1/4\)-net of size at most \(9^d\). For symmetric \(B\) and such a net \(\mathcal N\), \(\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq2\max_{x\in\mathcal N}|x^{\mathsf T}Bx|\). For part 2, consider the spaces \(V_i+V_j\).

3. The ground-state energy of a Gaussian spin system

For \(n\geq2\), let \((Z_{ij})_{1\leq j<i\leq n}\) be independent standard normal random variables. For \(\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n\), define \[ H_\sigma=\sum_{i>j}Z_{ij}\sigma_i\sigma_j, \qquad E_n=\min_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^n}H_\sigma. \]

  1. Prove that \(\mathbb EE_n=-\mathbb E\max_\sigma H_\sigma\) and that \(-\mathbb EE_n\leq Cn^{3/2}\).
  2. Construct signs successively by taking \(\sigma_1=1\) and choosing \(\sigma_i\) to maximize \(\sigma_i\sum_{j<i}Z_{ij}\sigma_j\). Condition on the previous rows to show that the expected value attained is \(\sqrt{2/\pi}\sum_{i=2}^n\sqrt{i-1}\). Deduce \(cn^{3/2}\leq-\mathbb EE_n\).
  3. Show that \(Z\mapsto E_n\) is \(\sqrt{n(n-1)/2}\)-Lipschitz, and deduce \[ \mathbb P\left\{\left|\frac{E_n}{\mathbb EE_n}-1\right|>\delta\right\} \leq2e^{-c\delta^2 n}\qquad(\delta>0). \] In particular, \(E_n/\mathbb EE_n\to1\) in probability.

Facts you may use. For centered Gaussian variables with variances at most \(v^2\), \(\mathbb E\max_{1\leq j\leq N}X_j\leq v\sqrt{2\log N}\), without any independence assumption. An \(L\)-Lipschitz function of a standard Gaussian vector satisfies \(\mathbb P\{|f-\mathbb Ef|>t\}\leq2e^{-t^2/(2L^2)}\).

4. Noisy measurements and feasible recovery

Let \(T\subset\mathbb R^n\) be nonempty and compact, let \(x\in T\), and let \(A\) be an \(m\times n\) standard Gaussian matrix. Observe \(y=Ax+\eta\), where \(\eta\in\mathbb R^m\) is deterministic. A reconstruction \(\widehat x\in T\) may depend measurably on \(A\) and \(y\).

You may use the following Gaussian matrix deviation bound: for every compact symmetric set \(S\subset\mathbb R^n\) containing \(0\), \[ \mathbb E\sup_{h\in S} \left|\|Ah\|_2-\sqrt m\|h\|_2\right|\leq Cw(S). \] No proof of this theorem is required.

  1. Show that \(w(T-T)=2w(T)\). If \(\|A\widehat x-y\|_2\leq\delta\) almost surely, where \(\delta\geq0\) is deterministic, prove \[ \mathbb E\|\widehat x-x\|_2 \leq\frac{Cw(T)+\|\eta\|_2+\delta}{\sqrt m}. \] The proof must allow \(\widehat x\) to be chosen after observing \(A\).
  2. Explain when exact interpolation (\(\delta=0\)) is feasible. For a constrained least-squares minimizer over \(T\), prove the bound with \(2\|\eta\|_2\) in place of \(\|\eta\|_2+\delta\).
  3. Specialize to \(T=RB_1^n\), \(R>0\), to obtain \(\mathbb E\|\widehat x-x\|_2\leq [CR\sqrt{\log(2n)}+2\|\eta\|_2]/\sqrt m\) for constrained least squares. Explain, by a kernel example when \(m<n\), why a feasibility restriction on \(\widehat x\) is essential.

Hint. Apply the deviation bound uniformly on \(T-T\) before substituting \(h=\widehat x-x\). For \(RB_1^n\), identify the supremum defining its width.

5. Random sections of the octahedron

Let \(1\leq d<n\), let \(A\) be a \(d\times n\) standard Gaussian matrix, and let \(E=\ker A\). Prove the Garnaev–Gluskin bound \[ \mathbb E\operatorname{diam}(B_1^n\cap E) \leq C\sqrt{\frac{\log(en/d)}{d}}, \] where diameter is measured in Euclidean distance. The following steps make the problem independent of later lectures.

  1. Put \(T_r=B_1^n\cap rB_2^n\). If \(n^{-1/2}\leq r\leq1\), prove \[w(T_r)\leq C\sqrt{\log(enr^2)}.\] You may use \(\mathbb E(\sum_{i=1}^s(g_i^*)^2)^{1/2} \leq C\sqrt{s\log(en/s)}\), where \(g_1^*\geq\cdots\geq g_n^*\) are the decreasing rearrangement of \(|g_1|,\ldots,|g_n|\).
  2. Write \(\operatorname{rad}(S)=\sup_{x\in S}\|x\|_2\). Prove that if \(\operatorname{rad}(T_r\cap E)\leq r/2\), then \(\operatorname{rad}(B_1^n\cap E)\leq r/2\).
  3. You may use the following Gaussian deviation theorem: for compact symmetric \(S\) containing \(0\) and \(u\geq0\), with probability at least \(1-2e^{-u^2}\), \[ \sup_{h\in S}\left|\|Ah\|_2-\sqrt d\|h\|_2\right| \leq C_0\big[w(S)+u\operatorname{rad}(S)\big]. \] Use it with \(S=T_r\), \(u=a\sqrt d\) for a sufficiently small absolute \(a>0\), and \(r=B\sqrt{\log(en/d)/d}\) for a sufficiently large absolute \(B\). Handle \(r\geq1\) separately. Deduce the asserted expectation bound, including the contribution of the exceptional event.

Hint for part 1. Split \(\langle g,x\rangle\) over the \(s\) largest coordinates of \(|g|\) and their complement, with \(s=\lceil r^{-2}\rceil\). The two contributions are at most \(r(\sum_{i\leq s}(g_i^*)^2)^{1/2}\) and \(g_{s+1}^*\), respectively; the second term is zero when \(s=n\).

6. Random approximation and entropy of a convex hull

Let \(v_1,\ldots,v_N\in B_2^n\), where \(N\geq1\), and set \(K=\operatorname{conv}\{0,\pm v_1,\ldots,\pm v_N\}\).

  1. For each \(x\in K\) and integer \(m\geq1\), construct independent random vectors \(Y_1,\ldots,Y_m\in\{0,\pm v_1,\ldots,\pm v_N\}\) with \(\mathbb EY_i=x\). Prove \[ \mathbb E\left\|m^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^mY_i-x\right\|_2^2\leq m^{-1}. \]
  2. Deduce, for \(0<\varepsilon\leq1\), \[ \log N(K,\|\cdot\|_2,\varepsilon) \leq \lceil\varepsilon^{-2}\rceil\log(2N+1). \] Explain why the approximating points belong to \(K\) and why the net works simultaneously for all \(x\in K\).
  3. Independently prove \(w(K)\leq C\sqrt{\log(2N)}\). Why does inserting just the entropy estimate from part 2 into Dudley’s integral fail to give a finite upper bound? Combine it with \(N(K,\varepsilon)\leq(1+2/\varepsilon)^n\) to explain why the actual entropy integral is nevertheless finite for every fixed \(n\).

Hint. For part 3, a linear functional attains its maximum over a finite convex hull at one of its vertices. Distinguish a divergent upper bound from a divergent integral.

7. Generic chaining on a binary tree

For a finite metric space \((T,d)\), define \[ \gamma_2(T,d)=\inf_{(T_j)}\sup_{t\in T} \sum_{j\geq0}2^{j/2}d(t,T_j), \qquad |T_0|=1,\quad |T_j|\leq2^{2^j}\ (j\geq1). \] You may use Talagrand’s theorem: for a centered Gaussian process with canonical metric \(d(s,t)=(\mathbb E|X_s-X_t|^2)^{1/2}\), \[ c\gamma_2(T,d)\leq\mathbb E\max_{t\in T}X_t\leq C\gamma_2(T,d). \]

Let \(T=\{0,1\}^m\), \(m\geq1\), be the leaves of a binary tree of depth \(m\). Attach independent standard Gaussian variables \(g_e\) to its edges and put \(X_t=\sum_{e\in[\varnothing,t]}g_e\).

  1. If \(\ell(s,t)\) is the length of the common prefix of two leaves, prove \(d(s,t)^2=2(m-\ell(s,t))\).
  2. Construct an admissible sequence by representing every prefix of length \(\min\{m,2^j\}\) at level \(j\geq1\) by one leaf, and taking a single leaf at level \(0\). Prove directly that \(\gamma_2(T,d)\leq Cm\).
  3. Starting at the root, repeatedly follow the edge with the larger Gaussian label. Use independence of unexplored edges to show that the expected value at the resulting leaf is \(m/\sqrt\pi\). Deduce \(\gamma_2(T,d)\geq cm\) and \(cm\leq\mathbb E\max_tX_t\leq Cm\).
  4. Prove \[ \mathbb P\left\{\left|\max_tX_t-\mathbb E\max_tX_t\right|>u\right\} \leq2e^{-u^2/(2m)}. \] Explain why, for \(m\geq2\), the leaves cannot be treated as independent Gaussian variables.

Hint. If \(a,b\) are independent standard normals, \(\max(a,b)=(a+b+|a-b|)/2\). In part 2, bound the nonzero sum by \(C\sqrt m\sum_{2^j<m}2^{j/2}\), keeping the level-zero term.

8. Product geometry and sub-Gaussian processes

Let \(a_1,\ldots,a_q>0\) and integers \(d_1,\ldots,d_q\geq1\) be given. In the orthogonal product \(\mathbb R^{d_1}\times\cdots\times\mathbb R^{d_q}\), let \[ T=\prod_{j=1}^q a_jB_2^{d_j} \] with its Euclidean metric. The definition and theorem in Problem 7 extend to compact Euclidean sets and their continuous Gaussian processes.

  1. Prove \[ w(T)=\sum_{j=1}^q a_j\mathbb E\|g^{(j)}\|_2, \qquad c\sum_{j=1}^q a_j\sqrt{d_j}\leq\gamma_2(T)\leq C\sum_{j=1}^q a_j\sqrt{d_j}, \] where \(g^{(j)}\) is standard Gaussian in \(\mathbb R^{d_j}\).
  2. Let \((Y_t)_{t\in T}\) be a centered process with almost surely continuous paths, \(Y_0=0\), and \[ \mathbb E e^{\lambda(Y_s-Y_t)} \leq\exp\left(\frac{L^2\lambda^2\|s-t\|_2^2}{2}\right) \quad(s,t\in T,\ \lambda\in\mathbb R). \] Using the generic chaining bound, prove \(\mathbb E\sup_{t\in T}|Y_t|\leq CL\sum_j a_j\sqrt{d_j}\).
  3. The diameter is \(2(\sum_j a_j^2)^{1/2}\). Compare this product with the Euclidean ball of that same radius \((\sum_j a_j^2)^{1/2}\) in dimension \(D=\sum_jd_j\). Show that bounding width only by this radius and \(D\) can lose an unbounded factor, using \(q=2\), \(d_1=1\), \(d_2=N\), \(a_1=1\), \(a_2=N^{-1}\).

Facts you may use. For a standard Gaussian vector in \(\mathbb R^d\), \(c\sqrt d\leq\mathbb E\|g\|_2\leq\sqrt d\). The generic chaining bound gives \(\mathbb E\sup_t|Y_t-Y_0|\leq CL\gamma_2(T)\) under the hypotheses in part 2.
